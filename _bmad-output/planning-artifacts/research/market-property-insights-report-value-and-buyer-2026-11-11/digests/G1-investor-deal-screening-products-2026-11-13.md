Evidence tags: Verified = primary vendor page read in full via fetch; Secondary = third-party article or search snippet; Unverified = single weak or conflicting source.

## Key findings

1. The sellable per-listing "investor screen" in Australia is thin. Microburbs is the only vendor found that screens actual for-sale listings on yield, forecast and risk, and even it shows only fit-to-filter on the listing, with substance behind a paid report. Cotality offers yield filters in search. No evidence of REA/PropTrack or Domain shipping listing-level investor metrics (not exhaustively checked).
2. Price ladders cluster by buyer type. Consumer-investor tiers: Microburbs $77-$320 per month (quarterly billing), Cotality Lite $180-$219 per month, HtAG ~$60-$150 per month (snippet). Research-membership tier is much higher: Hotspotting $3,000-$3,750 per quarter. US comparators are cheaper (Mashvisor $50-$100, DealCheck $14-$29, BiggerPockets $33-$39).
3. Per-report quotas (Microburbs: 5 to 30 property reports per month) show that a single report is the unit of value and the free tier is a funnel to it.
4. Most revenue for investor-facing brands does not come from software. Propertyology, Property Update and InvestorKit monetise through buyers-agent fees, lending, advice and mentoring; content and reports are lead generation. Hotspotting is the exception with a high-price membership.
5. What does not work or is fragile: Dashdot (buyers agent plus AI predictive model, "hundreds" of clients) collapsed in May 2026 and its tech was sold for a seven-figure sum, so predictive modelling alone did not carry the business; PropHero cut Australian staff within weeks of a A$25m raise and refocused offshore; Roofstock shrank by roughly half through two layoff rounds and pivoted; Mashvisor lifetime deals at $39.99 to $199.99 suggest heavy discounting and its reviews centre on data quality.
6. Traction evidence is almost absent. Microburbs, Hotspotting, HtAG, Cotality (investor tier), Propertyology and Property Update publish no subscriber counts on pages read. Only app-store style counts (DealCheck ~1.7K ratings) and BiggerPockets' community claim (3.5m) exist, and the latter is not a paying-user number.
7. Trust and data-quality are the recurring complaint in comparators (Mashvisor), and rent/yield estimates are the weakest, most disputed input; a product that shows its sources would be differentiated. (Inference from reviews; thin evidence.)
8. Pricing pages drift and contradict themselves (Microburbs structured data vs page, Mashvisor older vs current, Hotspotting two tiers), so any quoted price needs a date and a primary source.
