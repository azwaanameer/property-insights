# G2 - Investor purchase market size and investor research behaviour (digest, 2026-11-13)

Scope: Victoria and Australia property investor purchases; how investors research; spend on research/advice. Web-only (WebSearch + WebFetch). Access date for all sources: 2026-11-13.

Evidence status key: Verified = primary page fetched and read; Secondary = from a search-result summary or a third-party page, not read at the primary; Unverified = single weak/vendor source or could not be corroborated.

IMPORTANT unit note: ABS Lending Indicators count LOAN COMMITMENTS (a buyer can have several; refinancing is excluded from headline dwelling figures but "dwellings" includes construction of new, purchase of new, and purchase of existing dwellings). They are NOT counts of buyers or of completed purchases. ABS headline pages do not split "purchase of existing dwellings" for investors, and no Victorian investor counts were readable from the pages fetched (state tables are downloads).

## Key findings

1. Investor lending has collapsed in the last two quarters. ABS Sep qtr 2026: 35,301 investor commitments, down 30.6% on the quarter and 38.5% on the year, the "largest quarterly fall since the series began in September quarter 2002", third consecutive quarterly decline, broad-based across states. Value $24.7bn. This follows a peak around Sep qtr 2025 (57,624) and ~57.6k in Mar qtr 2026. Treat the 2025 peak as the pre-shock market and the Sep 2026 number as the new floor, not a baseline.

2. A policy shock, not just rates, is the likely driver. The 12 May 2026 Federal Budget restricts negative gearing on established residential property (losses only deductible against rental income/residential gains; from 1 July 2027) and replaces the 50% CGT discount with cost-base indexation and a 30% minimum tax, with new builds exempt/optioned and grandfathering for properties acquired before 7:30pm 12 May 2026 (Secondary: multiple news pages incl. realestatebusiness.com.au, thenewdaily.com.au, Forbes Australia, reported via search summaries; The Leader/Nov 2026 article says RBA Governor attributed the larger pullback to "something else going on"). Cotality's Tim Lawless is quoted estimating investors may fall from ~41% of demand to ~20% once settled (single news source, Unverified). Implication: the investor TAM for established-dwelling research is shrinking and shifting toward new builds (new-build share ~21% in Jun qtr 2026 and rising). Check the budget papers before using any "abolished" wording; sources differ between "abolished" and "restricted".

3. Investor share of dwelling commitments swung from ~40% (Sep 2025, Jun 2026 ~39%) to ~31.6% (Sep 2026) by count, per our derivation from ABS figures. Owner-occupier lending held up far better (-5.3% q/q).

4. Victoria: ABS pages read give only change and average loan size, not counts. Victoria investor commitments fell 14.2% in Jun qtr 2026 (NSW -15.5%) and owner-occupier commitments fell 6.6% in Sep qtr 2026. Victoria was the fastest-growing investor market in 2025 (+13% y/y reported). Victorian investor counts, and the existing-dwelling vs new split, must be taken from ABS Lending Indicators Table 14 (investors by state) and Table 16 (investors by detailed purpose, Victoria), which are downloads and were not opened. Nothing found on apartments vs houses for Melbourne investors.

5. Number of rental owners: ATO 2023-24 shows 1,672,616 individuals with a one-property rental interest (about 55% with a net rent loss) from a snippet; total owners across all property counts is not confirmed (the older figure of ~2.4m claimants is from 2020-21 via a secondary page). The number who buy a new investment property each year is NOT available directly. Proxy: investor commitments (not buyers) - e.g. ~210k-230k a year nationally at 2025 peak run-rate (4 x ~55k), which overstates buyers because one purchase can involve multiple loans and loans include construction and new dwellings. This is an inference, label it as such.

6. Buyers' agents: share of buyers using one is somewhere between 10% (realestate.com.au survey) and 30% (InfoTrack), 40% considered. For investors, a vendor-sourced figure suggests ~57% of first-time investors used some form of buyers' agent (34% local, 23% full-service) but that is Unverified. Fees: 2-3% + GST typical percentage fees; investor-oriented agents commonly flat $8k-$30k + GST. A free or low-cost report competes against a $10k-$25k adviser, which anchors price expectations at the high end for full service and at tens to low hundreds of dollars for data tools.

7. Willingness to pay for research: no robust survey found on the share of Australian investors who pay for research, or average spend. What exists are price points of paid providers (Hotspotting ~$1,980/yr; Oz Property Insights ~$15/month; CoreLogic/Cotality via RP Data Pro subscriptions) with no subscriber counts found. Cannot size a research-subscription market from data in hand; any estimate would need to be modelled bottom-up.

8. Short-stay is a niche: PIPA data point (year unconfirmed) has only 1.7% of last-year investor purchasers intending short-term accommodation use and 92.1% of surveyed investors with no STRA properties; Sydney Uni puts Airbnb-type listings at ~1.6% of housing stock (Dec 2024). A buyer-intent figure for Australian investors buying specifically for short stay is not available beyond this one survey; the higher (26%) survey is commercially biased.
