# I2 - Land, risk and amenity data sources for a Victorian address (digest)

Accessed: 2026-11-14 (all rows). Method: pages opened with WebFetch or curl; live WFS calls made to the Vicmap open data GeoServer on 2026-11-14. Dates are as printed on the page ("Last updated"); several DataVic pages show "Last updated" later than their metadata publish date, so treat those as the portal's last-touch date, not a content revision date.

Verdict key: Verified = licence text read on the publisher's page (or the file itself) and use is clear. Secondary = licence seen only via a search result or mirror, or the licence is clear but I could not open the primary page. Unverified = licence/terms not found or page blocked.

This is research, not legal advice. Items marked "(interpretation)" are my reading of the licence, to be checked by a lawyer before launch.

## 1. Headline findings

- The big win: Vicmap Property, Vicmap Planning, Vicmap Features of Interest, the Bushfire Prone Areas layer, the Victorian Heritage Register, GTFS and the Crime Statistics Agency tables are all published under Creative Commons Attribution (4.0 in nearly every case). The DEECA and DTP licensing pages say CC BY lets you "share, copy, redistribute (in any medium or format), adapt, remix, transform, and build upon the material for any purpose, even commercially". Attribution is the only condition. A paid report, a stored derived copy and a map display are all allowed for these.
- The big trap: Melbourne Water's own flood data (GIS Data Hub) is NOT open. Its Digital Data Licence Agreement bars "materials for sale", copying and keeping the data after the term. The statewide 1% AEP flood extent webservice (DEECA FloodZoom) has no open licence on its record and is marked "Limited Distribution". The safe flood route is the flood overlays (FO, LSIO, SBO) inside Vicmap Planning, which is CC BY.
- Apartments: the parcel layer holds one stacked polygon per lot. For plan PS437659 (Eureka Tower, 7 Riverside Quay) a live WFS count returned 867 lots, and the 500 I pulled all carried identical geometry. So lot count per plan is available, but per-lot land area is not (every lot shows the whole footprint). No area field exists on the parcel layers I checked; area must be computed from the polygon and is only meaningful for house lots.
- OSM and Overture: distances, counts and names shown in a report are very likely "Produced Work" territory under the ODbL (attribution only, no share-alike) as long as you do not publish a bulk extract of features. Storing a large extracted amenity table in your own database is the part that needs care (see section 4).
- NBN: no usable licensed address-level source. NBN Co's own website terms bar copying without written consent; the only open data is a March 2024 footprint map that says it must not be used to check availability at a premises.
- Not available from any source here: title ownership, last sale price of the lot, titled land area of an apartment, building footprints with licence suitable for sale outside OSM/Overture/Microsoft ODbL (Geoscape Buildings is the paid alternative; price not published).

## 3. Per-question answers

### (1) Parcel, land area, lot/plan, strata, building footprints
- Parcel boundary, lot/plan and SPI: yes, free, CC BY 4.0 (rows 1-4). Live test returned `parcel_spi` such as `BC47\PS437659`, `parcel_plan_number`, `parcel_lot_number`, `parcel_status`.
- Lot count of an apartment building: derivable. Count parcels by plan number (867 for PS437659). Caveat: one plan can cover several buildings or stages; counting is not the same as "units in the building" (see H5 digest for the G-NAF angle).
- Land area: no area attribute in v_parcel_mp or the parcel_view schema. For a house lot, compute planimetric area from the polygon (it will differ slightly from the titled area). For an apartment, every stacked lot repeated the same footprint, so a per-lot area cannot be derived. Titled lot area is not in the open data. Common property appears as its own lot in the plan; I did not isolate it.
- Building footprints: not in the Vicmap layers I tested. Free options are OSM and Overture (ODbL, see section 4); Overture buildings merge OSM and Microsoft ML with other sources. Geoscape Buildings is the paid, licensed alternative with heights, price unpublished. I did not check footprint completeness or quality in Victorian apartment areas.
- NOT available: title owner, title search, sale price of the lot, valuation (VGV is in other digests), mortgage, strata levies.

### (2) Planning, heritage, flood, bushfire
- Zones and all overlays: Vicmap Planning (row 6), weekly, CC BY 4.0.
- Heritage: local HO via Vicmap Planning overlays; state register via row 9.
- Bushfire: BMO via planning overlays; Bushfire Prone Area via row 8.
- Flood: use FO, LSIO, SBO overlays from Vicmap Planning (observed LSIO4 at the Eureka Tower point). The Melbourne Water dataset (row 12) and the DEECA statewide flood extent (row 11) are not safe for a paid product. Flood overlay polygons show a planning control, not a flood-risk probability; wording must say so.
- Other hazards (coastal inundation, contaminated land, etc.): not researched here; some DEECA coastal inundation datasets show CC BY 4.0 in search results (Secondary).

### (3) Crime
Row 13. Suburb and postcode incident counts yes (Table 03); rate per 100,000 only at LGA/PSA level; you compute suburb rates with ABS population. CC BY with CSA attribution.

### (4) Amenities
- Vicmap FOI: hospitals, libraries, schools, child care, police, fire, community venues, playgrounds, museums (row 7). No supermarket, shopping centre, gym or cafe.
- OSM / Overture Places fill the retail gaps. Overture Places licences are the permissive kind (CDLA Permissive 2.0, Apache 2.0, CC0) with no share-alike; Overture's ODbL themes are buildings, transport, divisions and base. This is a practical advantage for a POI table that you want to store and show without ODbL duties; confirm the CDLA clause text first.
- I did not measure OSM or Overture POI quality for Melbourne (supermarket and cafe completeness). That is a gap to test with a sample of addresses.

### (5) Public transport and walking
- Stops and timetables: PTV GTFS (rows 14-15), CC BY 4.0, covers trains, trams, buses.
- Walking distance and isochrones: no free hosted service allows commercial use (rows 26-27). Realistic path: self-host Valhalla (MIT) or similar on the Geofabrik Victoria extract and compute distances yourself. That keeps routing results as your own computed values, with the OSM caveats in section 4. Straight-line distance needs no routing service at all.

### (6) NBN and internet
Rows 23-25. Nothing licensed at address level is open. The March 2024 footprint map is CC BY 4.0 but says not to use it for a particular premises. A report line must be phrased as "NBN technology type varies; check your address at nbn" with no availability claim, unless you contract for NBN wholesale/third-party API access.

## 4. OSM and Overture: what the duties mean for a paid report

Quoted from the ODbL 1.0 text (opendatacommons.org):
- 4.3: "Creating and Using a Produced Work does not require the notice in Section 4.2." and "if you Publicly Use a Produced Work, You must include a notice associated with the Produced Work".
- 4.4: "Any Derivative Database that You Publicly Use must be only under the terms of:" (the ODbL or a compatible licence). Derivative Database means "a database based upon the Database, and includes any translation, adaptation, arrangement, modification" (text truncated on the page I read).
- 4.5: "You are not required to license Collective Databases under this License"; "Use of a Derivative Database internally within an organisation is not to the public".
- Produced Work: "a work (such as an image, audiovisual material, text, or sounds) resulting from using the whole" (or a substantial part) of the database.
- OSMF Produced Work guideline: "We can clearly define things that are USUALLY Produced Works: .PNG, JPG, .PDF, SVG images and any raster image". The same page says "if you publish a produced work, the underlying database has to be published as well" (a reference to ODbL 4.6, which applies to a Derivative Database that was used).
- OSMF Substantial guideline: an extraction is insubstantial if under 100 Features and one-off, or non-systematic and based on your own criteria, or for an area of up to 1,000 inhabitants; "we regard repeated small extractions as one big extraction!" Extracting "all eating places" in an area counts as systematic.
- OSMF Geocoding guideline: individual results "may be stored and used together with other proprietary or third party data"; but if results build a large database "this new database would be considered a Derivative Database".
- Attribution (OSMF): "Attribution must be to 'OpenStreetMap'"; in print/PDF "The URL to openstreetmap.org/copyright must be printed out".

What this means (interpretation, check with a lawyer):
1. Report output (PDF, web page) showing "Woolworths 320 m, Park 150 m" and a rendered map: Produced Work. Needs the OSM attribution and ODbL link on the map and in the PDF footer. No share-alike on the report.
2. Computing distances in your own code from OSM data held internally, and showing only the results: internal Derivative Database use is "not to the public", so no share-alike. Showing results per address is a Produced Work.
3. Risk point: storing a large, systematic extract of OSM features (all supermarkets, all cafes in Victoria) as your product database and exposing it publicly (an API, a downloadable list, or a map layer that lets users pull the dataset) is a Derivative Database, which triggers share-alike. A per-address report that shows a handful of items is not that, but a web product that renders a full amenity layer on a map for any address is nearer the line. Keeping the amenity table internal and exposing only per-address results is the conservative design.
4. Overture buildings, transport, divisions, base are ODbL so the same logic applies; Overture places (CDLA Permissive 2.0 etc.) carry attribution-style duties only for the listed sources. Add "(c) OpenStreetMap contributors, Overture Maps Foundation" when the data came from them.
5. Mixing OSM with CC BY Vicmap data in one database may create a combined database that is ODbL; keep layers in separate tables and in separate map layers (Collective Database pattern: independent databases are not required to be relicensed, per 4.5).

## 6. Blocked or unclear pages and open questions

- land.vic.gov.au pages returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch (Vicmap catalogue, Vicmap Property product description and data model PDFs). The same licensing page content was readable with a browser User-Agent on curl: "You can share, copy, redistribute (in any medium or format), adapt, remix, transform, and build upon the material for any purpose, even commercially." and "In general, data can be used for non-commercial purposes, but cannot be commercialised or on-sold" (the latter applies to departmental datasets not released via Data.Vic; check that each dataset you use is on DataVic with CC BY). Same wording on deeca.vic.gov.au: "A number of datasets are not available under Creative Commons ... the licence allows licensees to use the data for personal use or within their business but does not permit the data to be commercialised or on-sold." Pages: https://www.land.vic.gov.au/maps-and-spatial/spatial-data/how-to-access-spatial-data/licensing and https://deeca.vic.gov.au/maps/spatial-data/licensing (accessed 2026-11-14).
- DataShare terms page (https://datashare.maps.vic.gov.au/terms-of-use) says DEECA "owns the copyright in images and products downloaded from the Datashare website" and states "Terms and conditions apply when using DEECA's spatial data", with the licensing detail on another page. The DataVic CC BY dataset pages should govern, but DataShare orders may carry their own terms. Unverified conflict. The no-login WFS route avoids this.
- Melbourne Water agreement text was read from node/3256, which is the generic agreement; the hub may present a variant at download time.
- The CC BY 4.0 legal code was read (creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en): the grant has no NonCommercial wording; attribution is required in "any reasonable manner based on the medium, means, and context".
- Dataset page "Last updated" dates in DataVic are portal timestamps; I could not confirm content vintage of parcel or FOI layers.
- Not tested: Overture/OSM POI quality for Melbourne, Overture CDLA text, Geoscape pricing, NBN wholesale terms, ORS plans page, Mapbox product terms, VHD website terms, Victorian coastal and other hazard layers.
