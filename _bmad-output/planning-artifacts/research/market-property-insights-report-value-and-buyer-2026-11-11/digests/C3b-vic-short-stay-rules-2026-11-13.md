# C3b Victoria short-stay rules in force, November 2026 (dated 2026-11-13)

Status key: Verified = primary page read this session; Secondary = search-result summary or news/law-firm page not read in full; Unverified = not confirmed.
Budget used: 9 searches, 8 fetches (3 blocked/404: Maddocks 403, City of Melbourne minutes 403, lookupstrata 404).

## Rules

1. State registration scheme. No separate statewide short-stay registration scheme for properties was found. The only state registration is SRO registration for the Short Stay Levy: platforms register (not per property); owners/tenants register only if they take direct (non-platform) bookings. Start 1 Jan 2025. Status: Verified for levy registration (SRO page, updated 29 Sep 2026); "no other scheme" is Secondary (absence in searches; one document mentions a possible statewide register of short-stay landlords as a proposal only, Unverified).
   Source: https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/owning-property/short-stay-levy/understanding-short-stay-levy (accessed 2026-11-13)
2. Short Stay Levy. 7.5% of total booking fee (nightly rate, cleaning fees, GST, late checkout fee; excludes payment-processing and damage charges). Applies to stays under 28 consecutive days in the same property (checkout day not counted). Start 1 Jan 2025. Platform pays for platform bookings; owner/tenant pays for direct bookings. Lodgement: $75,000+ in booking fees per calendar year = quarterly (30 days after quarter end); under $75,000 = annually by 30 January. Status: Verified.
   Exclusions: principal place of residence; part of home not separately occupiable; commercial residential premises (hotels, motels, hostels); movable houseboats. Exemptions: staff housing within a broader facility, rooming houses, retirement villages, residential care/supported residential services, student accommodation linked to an educational institution, funded non-profit crisis accommodation. Status: Verified. Same source as above. Penalties and booking-date vs stay-date trigger not stated on the page (Unverified; secondary sources say booking accepted on/after 1 Jan 2025).
3. Owners corporation (OC) ban. From 1 Jan 2025 (Short Stay Levy Act 2024 amending the Owners Corporations Act 2006) an OC can make a rule banning use of lots for short stay (booking under 28 days). Needs special resolution (75% of lot owners; 75% of lot entitlements if by ballot/poll). Cannot apply to a lot that is the owner's or occupier's principal place of residence. Rule can have a future start date; page advises managing existing bookings; no general grandfathering described. Status: Verified.
   Source: https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/owners-corporations/rules/making-rules-to-ban-short-stay-accommodation (page updated 8 Jan 2025; accessed 2026-11-13)
   The page does not mention restrictions short of a ban (night limits) or OC fees/charges for short stays. A secondary source says a blanket ban is not permitted; that conflicts with the CAV page and is Unverified.
4. OC/VCAT conduct powers (since Feb 2019, amended Jan 2025). OC can issue breach notices for guest conduct; owner and guests can be jointly liable. VCAT can order fines up to $1,100, compensation up to $2,000 per affected resident, and stop an apartment being rented short-stay for a period; after three breach notices within 24 months for separate events VCAT can restrict the apartment from short-stay letting. Status: Verified.
   Source: https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/owners-corporations/rules/short-stay-accommodation-laws-to-prevent-unruly-parties (updated 4 Feb 2025; accessed 2026-11-13). Page does not say whether OCs may charge fees for short stays.
5. 2026 OC Act changes. Secondary reports say the expert panel review recommended a statewide cap of two people per bedroom for short stays; the government did not support it, and also did not support aligning the "principal place of residence" definition across the OC Act and Short Stay Levy Act; it cited the Short Stay Levy Act 2024 as sufficient. Status: Secondary (lookupstrata.com.au/victorian-strata-laws-2026-whats-changing; southbanklocalnews.com.au; primary government response not retrieved). A notice says Owners Corporations Amendment Regulations 2026 commence 25 November 2026; content and any short-stay effect not confirmed (Unverified; https://www.vic.gov.au/notice-decision-16 appeared in search only).
6. City of Melbourne. At the Future Melbourne Committee on 11 June 2024 councillors unanimously paused the draft short-stay local law (registration fee about $350 and 180-day cap had been floated) because they concluded the state levy would override it; they resolved to keep engaging with the state and monitor the levy. Status: Secondary (news, published 18 June 2024: https://themelburnian.au/council-short-stay-rental-cap-plan-scrapped-as-government-to-introduce-levy/). One secondary 2026 guide says no City of Melbourne cap or registration is in force; I found no later council decision. Current position Unverified against council records (council minutes PDF blocked, 403).
7. Planning. Secondary sources say the 2024 reforms let councils opt in to requiring a planning permit for short stays via a planning scheme amendment approved by the Planning Minister; councils cannot ban outright; councils can access SRO levy data for their municipality. I found no evidence that City of Melbourne has gazetted such an amendment. Status: Secondary/Unverified (aol.co.uk and urbandeveloper.com summaries; no planning.vic.gov.au page found).
8. Public register or tool for a building. None found. The SRO register is of levy registrants, not buildings. Under the OC Act a lot owner or prospective buyer can request the OC register and rules (OCs record rules; one strata site says rules should be registered with Land Use Victoria). Status: Unverified (absence of a public building-level register is based on searches only). In June 2024 the Lord Mayor planned to ask the Treasurer for a register of short-stay locations and rental days; no outcome found.

## What this means for a buyer of an apartment (facts only)
- Whether a building allows short stays depends on that building's OC rules, not on a public state or City of Melbourne list; the OC can ban (75% special resolution) any time since 1 Jan 2025 and can revoke or change it the same way.
- An OC ban does not apply to a lot that is the owner's or occupier's principal place of residence.
- Short stays under 28 days attract a 7.5% levy on booking fees (platform pays for platform bookings; owner/tenant pays for direct bookings; principal residence excluded).
- No statewide cap on nights and no City of Melbourne registration or cap were found in force.
- VCAT can bar a lot from short-stay letting after three breach notices in 24 months.
- A buyer can ask the OC (via the OC certificate/register) for current rules; the OC certificate content was not checked in this research.
