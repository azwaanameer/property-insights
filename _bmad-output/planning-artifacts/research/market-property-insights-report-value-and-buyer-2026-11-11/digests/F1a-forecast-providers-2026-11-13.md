## Key findings

1. **Microburbs is the only provider found that publishes suburb-level numeric forecasts WITH a backtest and a sellable API.** It is also the only one that discloses a (partial) method (mean reversion vs similar suburbs). Everything about its accuracy is self-measured; no independent audit exists in what I found.
2. **The Microburbs evidence is honest about being modest.** Its own test shows top-5 picks ~+7.3% over 2y (95% range +5.5% to +8.7%), the edge concentrated at the very top of the list (top 10% only ~+2.8%), individual-suburb correlation 0.21, "calibration roughly unbiased but individual suburbs vary widely", and "The forecast ranks and times. The report vets." The headline stat is for houses only; units are untested. So a forecast is a shortlisting signal, not a valuation, and is weak for any single suburb. Its marketing track-record page uses stronger-looking numbers (28% vs 9%) that have gaps (missing months, 55 of 209 top-10 calls behind) and mixes two different test designs.
3. **Microburbs terms probably block the intended use.** Standard API terms forbid redistribution to third parties and resale, require attribution on every view, cap caching at 30 days and restrict display to authenticated users. Putting suburb forecasts into a PDF sold to a buyer would need a custom agreement (the terms say contact the founder). Treat forecast embedding as a licence negotiation, not an API purchase.
4. **The big data houses do not sell suburb forecasts in Australia (as far as found).** Cotality's suburb report is historical only (verified); Domain, PropTrack and SQM publish capital-city forecasts. This is absence of evidence from searches, but consistent across sources. Their city forecasts also visibly moved: SQM cut its 2026 base case from +6-10% to 0 to +3% within months; Domain flipped from +6% houses to Sydney/Melbourne falls for FY27. Short-horizon forecasts in this market have been unstable.
5. **Hotspotting, Propertyology and most advisers offer methodology-by-narrative with only self-claimed hits** ("proven bang on", "consistently predicted booms") and no figures, dates or denominators. Hotspotting's one disclosed input (rising sales volumes) is sensible but not tested publicly.
6. **Track-record claims in this market are mostly self-graded and selection-biased.** HtAG's "135 of 135" were chosen from 5,000+ candidates and graded by HtAG; Microburbs critiques it while grading itself. Microburbs also notes a naive "prices rise every year" model scores ~73% direction 2010-2024, so hit-rate claims need a baseline. An important point for a downturn year: most backtests cover a rising-market era (the Microburbs test window ends Feb 2024; the live record ends Jun 2025), and nothing found tests the current falling market.
7. **AI/ML startups: nothing local and independent found.** Microburbs published an LLM test (GPT suburb picks lagged, e.g. a +10% pick fell 18.1% over 3y; only 12.7% of WA picks beat the state median - vendor claim). PropertyIQ is the only quantified ML scoring product found but it is US. HtAG is the other data-science-style Australian vendor (Growth Rate Cycle, API with 87 endpoints, MCP).
8. **Implication for a report product**: an honest option is to present relative, ranked, "momentum/signal" context with stated uncertainty (or to derive transparent in-house indicators), rather than a point forecast attributed to a vendor. Any vendor-attributed forecast needs written licence terms.
