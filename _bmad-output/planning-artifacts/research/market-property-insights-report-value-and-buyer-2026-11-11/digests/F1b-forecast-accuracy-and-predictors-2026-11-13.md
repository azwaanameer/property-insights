# F1b: Forecast accuracy and predictors of suburb price growth / rental return (2026-11-13)

Status key: Verified = primary page fetched and read; Secondary = reported by a search summary or another article; Unverified = not opened.
Independence key: INDEP = academic/central bank/regulator; VENDOR = commercial firm selling data/forecasts; ADVOCACY = commentator/promoter/adviser.
Budget used: 12 searches, 6 fetches (1 blocked). Overall: independent evidence is thin and mostly city/national; suburb-level predictor evidence is almost entirely vendor-published and unaudited.

## 2. Forecast accuracy evidence

- No independent (peer-reviewed or central bank) backtest of Australian suburb-level price forecasts was found. No Cotality, PropTrack, or Domain self-audit found either. (Domain notes its year-end figures blend actual and forecast, which complicates self-scoring.)
- National consensus (Microburbs, VENDOR, read in full, unreviewed): January consensus forecasts 2010-2024, 115+ forecasts, 20+ institutions, vs CoreLogic national index. Direction accuracy reported 81% (13/16) but its table shows 12/15 (80%), so figures do not reconcile. Always-up baseline scores 75%; author concedes the gap is not statistically significant. MAE ~5.5pp (my calculation). Wrong direction in 2019, 2020, 2023. Author claims magnitude is not reliably predictable, policy-driven reversals unpredictable, scenario ranges beat point forecasts (n=2). Claim that actuals exceeded consensus every year conflicts with years of price falls; treat bias claim cautiously. National only; no apartment breakdown.
- Anecdotal misses (HotSpotting/Terry Ryder, ADVOCACY): NAB forecast Sydney houses -5.6% for 2019 vs outcome +4.8 to +6.8%; ANZ forecast -13% capital city prices for 2020 vs ~+25% nationally by end-2021. Selective, and Ryder sells hotspot research.
- RBA RDP 2019-01 (INDEP, Verified): publishes no out-of-sample forecast errors. Rents are easy to forecast; prices partly predictable via momentum and slow adjustment; scenario of ~0.2%/yr real price growth over a decade under lower price expectations.
- Hotspot lists: RiskWise (above): about a third of listed hotspots met the benchmark.
- Microburbs test of GPT/LLM suburb picks: 17,096 predictions underperformed by 0.96pp (VENDOR, Secondary); not a test of human experts.
- Horizon: no source defines a predictable horizon. Implied: rents and vacancy are forecastable over 1-3 yrs (persistence); city price direction over 12 months is right ~75-80% of the time in the one tally but barely beats "always up"; magnitude and turning points are not reliably forecastable; any suburb ranking edge shown by vendors is over 2-4 yr windows, houses mostly, unaudited.

## 3. Key findings

1. Interest rates are the dominant driver of city price swings (RBA); suburb forecasts are conditional on a rate path nobody predicts well, hence large misses at 2019-2023 turning points.
2. National consensus forecasts called direction ~75-80% of years, barely above an always-up baseline; magnitude is poor (MAE ~5.5pp on the single vendor tally). Treat price point-forecasts as low-value.
3. Rents, vacancy and supply are the best-grounded measurable indicators: rents are highly persistent and vacancy elasticity is quantified (about 2pts rent growth per 1pt vacancy); +1% dwelling stock lowers rents and prices ~2.5%. This supports reporting rental-side and supply-pipeline metrics for apartments rather than price forecasts.
4. Hotspot lists and composite suburb picks have a weak record where independently checked (RiskWise: 33% of unit hotspots beat the market); vendor backtests claiming edge are unaudited, houses-only in the one paper read, and use overlapping windows.
5. Suburb momentum is unreliable: vendor evidence points to mean reversion (+3.0%/yr over 4 yrs) in one dataset but the HtAG cohort correlation flips sign between cycles. Do not use "past growth" as a signal either way.
6. Infrastructure effects are real but local (within ~1.4 km), modest for units (about 2-7% vs ~9% for houses on level crossing removals), and the Sydney Northwest Metro evidence shows announcement-stage effects can be negative. No evidence on Metro Tunnel or SRL price effects, or on persistence.
7. Melbourne units underperformed houses for roughly a decade, linked to oversupply and rate-driven house outperformance; off-the-plan and investor-heavy small-unit stock shows high resale-loss rates. The recent unit rebound is one to a few quarters and vendor-sourced.
8. The honest message for a report: independent evidence supports describing current conditions (yield, vacancy, supply pipeline, investor share, resale-loss rates) and uncertainty, not predicting suburb rankings.

- Level crossing study was unpublished when The Conversation article ran (2019); published version, if any, not checked. Persistence of effects unknown.
- No peer-reviewed suburb-level backtest, no apartment-specific predictor regression, no Docklands/Southbank-specific price study, no study on owner-occupier share or gentrification proximity as predictors, no SRL or Metro Tunnel price study.
- Blake and Gharleghi, AHURI and RDP 2014-09 findings are from search-result summaries, not read in full.
- Microburbs report arithmetic inconsistencies (13/16 vs 12/15; bias claim) noted; vendor numbers should not be cited as fact without caveat.
