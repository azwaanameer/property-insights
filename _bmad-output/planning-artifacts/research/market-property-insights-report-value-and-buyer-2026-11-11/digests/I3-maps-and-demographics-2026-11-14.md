## Q1. Maps

### 1.1 Recommendation (my inference from the verified facts below)

1. Web page: MapLibre GL JS plus a Protomaps PMTiles extract for Victoria (or Australia) in a Cloudflare R2 bucket. Every licence in that chain is permissive or open-data, commercial use is allowed, and R2 has no egress fee. Cost is storage plus per-request fees only. Verified (licences and R2 price); the size of a Victoria extract was not found (Unverified).
2. PDF: render the static map images yourself at PDF build time (headless browser running MapLibre GL JS against the same PMTiles file, or MapLibre Native). The map is then your own Produced Work from ODbL data, so there is no per-image fee and no vendor print licence. Whether MapLibre Native resolves `pmtiles://` in a Node or headless build was not confirmed (Unverified); the headless-browser route is the lower-risk path. This is a design suggestion, not a verified claim.
3. Do not build the PDF on OpenStreetMap's tile server or OpenFreeMap's public instance (see rows below).
4. Do not use Mapbox for the PDF unless you buy print rights (Mapbox Product Terms 1.7). Do not use Google Static Maps inside a stored PDF without written confirmation from Google (see 1.3).
5. Aerial or satellite: there is no open statewide aerial imagery for Victoria. Plan for no imagery in v1, or pay for it (see 1.4).

### 2.3 How small counts are treated

- ABS DataPacks readme: "Please note that there are small random adjustments made to all cell values to protect the confidentiality of data. These adjustments may cause the sum of rows or columns to differ by small amounts from the table totals." Verified (file in our pack).
- ABS Community Profiles page: "Small random adjustments have been made to all cell values to protect the confidentiality of respondents." Verified.
- ABS "How the data is processed" (Census 2021 methodology, data released 28/06/2022): "all values are slightly adjusted, including the totals values in tables" and "No reliance should be placed on small cells as they are impacted by random adjustment, respondent and processing errors." It does not mention suppression and points to a separate Data confidentiality guide, which I did not open. Verified for the quotes.
- ABS QuickStats for a very small suburb (Gazette, SAL20972) shows: "Due to the small population for this area, limited information has been provided." and "Median and average values may be affected by confidentiality in small areas." Verified. ABS itself drops the country, ancestry, religion and language panels for that suburb.
- What our file shows (my own checks on 2021_GCP_SAL_VIC.zip): in G01 and G14, the values 1 and 2 never occur as a cell value; counts jump from 0 to 3 (G14: 184,620 zero cells, none at 1 or 2, 8,285 at 3). 109 suburbs have a total population of 0. 122 suburbs have a G14 total of 1 to 9 people. Example: SAL20040 shows total persons 4, born elsewhere 5, born in Australia 4. This is consistent with ABS random adjustment but the exact algorithm is not published in anything I opened (the one outside paper I saw says the same, Secondary). Whether any cells are suppressed rather than adjusted: the GCP files use "0" and ".." (not applicable) and I found no blank or "np" cells in G01, G08, G13A or G14, so no suppression markers were seen (Verified locally for those four files only).
- Licence of the pack: CC BY 4.0. Attribution text in the pack: "Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics" for unmodified material, or "Based on Australian Bureau of Statistics data" for derived percentages, plus "ABS data used with permission from the Australian Bureau of Statistics" and a link to www.abs.gov.au. Verified (CreativeCommons_Licensing_readme.txt in our pack).

Practical rule for the product (my recommendation): show a count or share only above a population floor (for example 100 residents for any ancestry, birthplace or religion row), show "not shown, small population" below it, and print "Small random adjustments have been made to all cell values" on the page. The 100-resident floor is my number, not an ABS rule; the .id blog (Secondary, via search) says profile.id generally requires 1,000 people for small areas.

**What the law does and does not say (my analysis, for a lawyer to confirm).** The text read bites on acts of refusing, treating less favourably, advertising an intention to discriminate, and assisting or promoting such acts. Publishing Census counts is not itself any of those acts, and the same tables are published by the ABS under CC BY 4.0. The exposure comes from design: a product that ranks, filters or scores suburbs by ethnic or religious makeup, or that markets itself as helping buyers find or avoid communities, can be characterised as steering or as "assisting or promoting" discrimination by an agent user (s 17), and a listing-style page that signals a preference could be read under s 16 or EO Act s 182. Those readings are untested here: Unverified.

**What a careful product should and should not show (my recommendation, drawn from the above)**

Should:
- Show ABS facts as plain descriptive tables with "Source: ABS Census 2021, Table G14" style citations and the CC BY attribution text.
- Add a short neutral note: "Census figures describe who lived in this suburb on Census night 2021. They say nothing about any individual, and are not a guide to who may buy or rent here."
- Use a population floor and the ABS adjustment note (2.3).
- Label ancestry as multi-response.
- Keep these panels on the page and PDF as a "Community profile" section, separate from the scoring pages.

Should not:
- Rank, rate, score, colour-code, map as heatmaps, sort, or compare suburbs by ethnicity, ancestry, birthplace, language or religion.
- Feed any of G08, G09, G10, G13, G14 or Indigenous status (G07) into the rating, screener, "similar suburbs" match, or any text generation (for example "a diverse community" as a selling point).
- Offer filters or alerts by those fields, or describe them in agent-facing or listing-style copy.
- Show them to the street or small-area level, or as modelled estimates.
- Say "known for its X community".
