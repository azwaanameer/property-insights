### Regulatory accountability

5. Best interests duty (National Credit Act; ASIC RG 273): brokers must act in the consumer's best interests when giving credit assistance and prioritise the consumer where there is a conflict. A disclaimer or client consent cannot discharge it; the duty covers conduct and processes, not only product suitability. Separate from and additional to responsible lending. Licensees must take reasonable steps to prevent breaches. Search-summary only (ASIC pages surfaced, not read). https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/regulatory-guides/rg-273-mortgage-brokers-best-interests-duty | ASIC | RG 273 June 2020 | accessed 2026-11-11 | medium-high | regulatory
6. ASIC REP 840 (reviewed 13 licensees across 8 aggregator groups): broker practices generally aligned with guidance, but loan recommendation practices, supervision and complaints handling could be strengthened; key concern was recommending a loan when a lower-cost alternative was available. ASIC says its sample was targeted, not prevalence evidence. Search-summary only. https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/reports/rep-840-mortgage-brokers-review-of-best-interests-duty-and-complaints-handling-practices | ASIC (media release 26-240MR) | Oct 2026 (see Contradictions) | accessed 2026-11-11 | medium | regulatory
   - Relevance: documentation and file-evidence is a live ASIC theme. Nothing found that says property research must be documented, so a property brief as "evidence on file" is a hypothesis, not a finding.

### Lender apartment policy (Melbourne-relevant)

14. Macquarie (primary broker policy page): density is set by POSTCODE not building; max LVR 80% in high-density postcodes; 70% if any portion is interest-only; off-the-plan max 90% (80% in high-density postcodes); studio/small unit may be considered if over 40 sqm internal with separate bedroom, with comparable sales under 50 sqm required, and BDM check advised; upfront valuation must be ordered through PropertyHub before submission. Fetched. https://www.macquarie.com.au/help/brokers/credit-guidelines-and-processes/understanding-who-and-what-we-finance/understanding-acceptable-securities-and-maximum-lvr.html | Macquarie Bank | undated (page shows no date; freshness vs 12-month rule NOT verified) | accessed 2026-11-11 | medium | policy
15. Broker-written lender summary (secondary, checked "2 August 2026", updated 28 Oct 2026): lenders may decline units under their minimum floor area (internal area excluding balcony/parking/storage); postcodes can be classed high density/restricted; per-development exposure limits can cause a decline even when the borrower qualifies; valuation can come in below price, requiring more deposit; developer rebates are treated as reducing price per APRA. Names building-register/dwelling-count rules at Westpac, St George, CBA, NAB, ANZ and size/postcode/concentration rules at Bankwest, ING, Macquarie, AMP, People First. No numeric thresholds given. Fetched. https://www.huntergalloway.com.au/high-density-apartment-home-loan/ | Hunter Galloway (broker firm) | 2026-10-28 | accessed 2026-11-11 | medium (secondary, marketing register) | policy
16. Other lender details surfaced via search summary only (page dates unknown, older than 12 months possible): Bendigo defines high density as more than 5 storeys or more than 50 units, with 80% LVR (no LMI)/90% (with LMI) cap; Resimac lists Melbourne 3000 and 3008 as high-density for specialist products. https://www.brokers.bendigobank.com.au/siteassets/_documents/guides/security-and-lvr.pdf | Bendigo Bank | undated | accessed 2026-11-11 | low (stale risk) | policy
   - Pattern: apartment acceptability is lender-specific, postcode- and building-size-driven, and changes often; a per-address brief that states building scale, internal size, postcode density class and comparable evidence aligns with what brokers must check, but I found no tool that maps a specific address to each lender's policy result.

### Pain points

17. Valuation pain (Australian broker voice, Feb 2025, so just inside the 2-year behaviour window): inconsistent valuations, outdated or irrelevant sales evidence, unwillingness to revise, below-market valuations derailing deals, low valuations pushing borrowers to higher LVR, "unnecessary" full valuations causing delay; the broker proposed sales evidence no older than 3 months. Fetched. https://www.theadviser.com.au/broker/46673-broker-lodges-petition-to-hold-property-valuers-to-account | The Adviser (Momentum Media) | 2025-02-11 | accessed 2026-11-11 | medium (one broker's petition) | sentiment
18. Coverage via search summary: valuation-management firm ValEx has argued brokers should stop disputing low valuations because it seldom changes outcomes; workaround advice is to pre-research comparable sales and challenge with supporting sales evidence, or use lenders that do not require a valuation. One Domain article cites an unofficial estimate that at least 35% of declined loans are due to low valuations (unverified, undated, likely old). https://www.brokernews.com.au (ValEx piece) and https://www.domain.com.au/advice/can-your-loan-fall-through | Broker News; Domain | undated | accessed 2026-11-11 | low | sentiment
   - Link to product: comparable-sale evidence assembled BEFORE a valuation is the one workaround repeatedly advised, which matches the proposed brief. Whether brokers do this with existing tools (RP Data already gives comparables) is unknown.

- ASIC REP 840 date: ASIC report page reportedly says 13 October 2026; MFAA article reportedly says 12 October 2026. Unresolved, trivial.
- A custom-dev vendor page claimed no white-label real estate dashboard product exists, contradicting vendor listings (Cotality, PropTrack, Microburbs); the claim is from a dev-sales page, discounted.
