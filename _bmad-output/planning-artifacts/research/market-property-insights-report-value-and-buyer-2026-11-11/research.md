# Market research: what a Melbourne apartment insights report is worth, and to whom

**Decision this research serves:** What can City Retreats sell as a property-insights report, to whom, and why would they pay when realestate.com.au and Domain show a lot for free?

## Executive summary

**Do not sell same-building sales, rents and yield as the core product. Domain already gives them away.** Domain's free Building Profile for 628 Flinders St (one 700-unit tower; a "Log in" link was visible, suggesting a logged-out view) shows the building's sold prices, per-unit capital growth, rental yield on recent sales, the owner/renter split, how long owners have stayed, and current listings [1]. By our internal comparison, that is most of sections 2 to 4 of our mock-up (not evidenced in this research). All six sales we could compare carry identical prices on both portals, with one date two weeks apart [41].

**What is still not given away, per everything we could read:**
1. **Building condition and finances.** Owners corporation fees, levies, sinking fund, defects. The statutory certificate is capped at $166.48 to $299.63 [9], and the one manager checked charges exactly the cap [10], so a retrieval service has no markup room. A report could interpret the certificate. Cohabit says it has extended to Victoria and targets this gap, but that is vendor press only: we found no Victorian product page, date or price [11].
2. **Short-stay income and permission.** Estimated yearly income, what already operates in the building, and whether the building allows it. No free tool we read shows this [1][3]. We hold AirDNA data and a building register (project data, not part of this research).
3. **A decision, not a data dump.** One page that combines comps, suburb rent and growth, planning, new supply and the gaps, with the questions to ask next. The free tools show data; none we read explains it.

**Who to aim at first, in order of evidence:**
- **Short-stay operators and investors** (your own market): no free tool we read shows short-stay income or building activity, and you hold that data. *Demand for it was not researched; this needs its own round.*
- **Apartment investors and buyer's agents** wanting building due diligence: pain is real and official [28], price anchors exist ($43 to $600 per report; $77 to $410 a month), but **we found no first-person statement of willingness to pay**.
- **Mortgage brokers last.** The channel is huge (81.6% of new loans [14]) but Cotality already gives brokers a branded client report inside RP Data Premium/Pro, which not all brokers hold [6], and we found no evidence of brokers buying per-address reports from a startup.

**Growth data:** free official suburb medians exist and can be reused with attribution (quarterly unit medians published by the Department of Transport and Planning from Valuer-General Victoria sales data, CC BY 4.0 [19]; Homes Victoria rents, loaded from the primary file: Docklands 2-bed flat $800 a week, +6.7% on the year [38]). Cotality's terms appear to require a licence to reuse its growth figures; confirm in writing [7][8]. **We found no suburb-level apartment forecast in the sources checked, and no independent evidence that forecasts are accurate. Do not publish projections yet.**

**Biggest caveats.** Evidence on what customers will pay is thin and mostly vendor-sourced. The realestate.com.au free pages could not be read at all. Rights to REA and Domain data were not established (their terms could not be retrieved). Take the licensing and advice-regulation points to a lawyer before launch.

## D1. What the free portals show

**Verdict:** the free offer is broader than we assumed, including at building level.

| Source | What a free user gets | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Domain Building Profile [1] | Same-building sold prices; per-unit capital growth since last purchase (e.g. unit 413 sold $445,000 in Nov 2026 against $582,000 in Jun 2016, -2.55% a year); gross yield for recent sales ($640 to $800 a week rents against $388k to $570k prices); 45%/55% owner/renter split; owner tenure; 23 other for-sale listings plus for-rent and 29 more recent sold/rented entries | High for this building (first-hand paste; "Log in" link visible, logged-out view unconfirmed); other buildings not checked |
| ANZ free emailed property report [2] | Sales and rental history, similar nearby sales (area-based, not same-building), a PropTrack price range, suburb 5-year house/unit price change | High |
| Domain property profile | Estimate range with confidence rating, sold and rented history | Medium to low (snippet only) |
| Landchecker free report [3] | Planning zone, overlays, bushfire; sales history is in the paid report ($24.90) | High |
| realestate.com.au | Could not be retrieved; which fields are free is unknown | None |

**Not shown on the Domain building page or any free tool we read:** owners corporation fees or levies, building age, short-stay rules, defects, planning overlays, short-stay income, supply pipeline [1]. Absence on the pages read is not proof of absence everywhere.

**Data-quality finding for us:** up to 4 of the 10 recent entries on Domain's list are absent from our REA-via-wrapper harvest (units 413, 1914, 3402, 1412). Three of them carry a price; 1914 shows no price and also sits in the for-sale list, so it may not be a completed sale [41]. Domain's newest priced sale, 413 at $445,000 (9 Nov 2026), is below the five like-for-like sales we used (lowest $495,888). Adding it would lower our median from $510,000 to about $505,000 (six values, tiny sample) [1][41].

## D3. What investors and buyers say they struggle with

Evidence is thin: first-person voice came from one Reddit thread and one old Whirlpool snippet; most else is vendor, adviser or media [29].

- **Victorian pre-contract information is limited.** Cohabit says the OC certificate has about 3 items against 14 interstate [11] (**vendor-framed, unverified**). Official Consumer Affairs Victoria checklists tell buyers to dig into minutes, the sinking fund, levy history, proposed works and special levies themselves [28][34].
- **Fear of special levies and an empty sinking fund** appeared in a Melbourne investor thread and an older Whirlpool post (snippet only; each a single thread; low confidence) [29][37]. Cladding costs are a documented precedent [31].
- **Buyers skip the reports that exist:** a Sydney strata lawyer is quoted in a headline saying up to 90% do (anecdotal, NSW, headline only) [30].
- **Workaround seen:** a building report costing "a few hundred" dollars, and joining the committee after purchase [29].
- **Not found:** any first-person willingness-to-pay statement, any survey of research tools used, any investor reaction to Victoria's 2025 short-stay rules.

**Forecasts:** no suburb-level apartment forecast was found in the sources checked (SQM pages could not be read; Domain, PropTrack and Cotality forecasts were not checked). **Free growth for our six suburbs is likely obtainable, but coverage of those suburbs is unchecked.** The quarterly files have not been opened: the state site blocks scripted downloads, so they must be fetched in a browser.

## D5. Mortgage brokers

- **Channel:** brokers settled 81.6% of new home loans in the June 2026 quarter (verified) [14]; about 24,116 brokers (**unverified**: the count appeared on a page one researcher read but not on the page a verifier checked) [15]; median gross broker revenue about $127,000 [15].
- **Incumbent substitute:** Cotality's Digital Property Report gives brokers a logo-branded client report with AVM sale and rent estimates and comparables, bundled with RP Data subscriptions, no price published [6]. PropTrack and Microburbs also offer branded reports [35][36].
- **Apartment lending policy is the real broker pain:** postcode-based high-density limits, minimum sizes (Macquarie: small units over 40 sqm internal may be considered, 80% maximum LVR in high-density postcodes, undated page) and per-development limits differ by lender [16][18]. No tool was found that maps a specific address to each lender's result. This is an idea, not evidence of demand.
- **Valuation pain:** one broker petition (Feb 2025) cites inconsistent valuations and stale sales evidence [17]; trade advice (low confidence) is to gather comparables before the valuation [33].
- **Not found:** any broker buying per-address reports from a startup; any broker interview; prices of broker tools.

## D6. Rights and risk (for a lawyer, not legal advice)

- **REA and Domain terms could not be retrieved** (404 and 403). Whether they allow commercial reuse is **unknown**. Cotality, a comparable, expressly bans scraping and aggregation [7].
- **Enforcement seen:** REA sued Domain in the Federal Court in 2024 over 181 scraped listings, claiming IP in photos and floorplans; no ruling found [27] (**unverified**). Law-firm commentary (not read directly) says breach of website terms is the likeliest scraping risk, and that copyright bites on photos and text more than on facts.
- **Project fact: our REA data comes through an unofficial marketplace wrapper.** No Australian analysis of that route was found. Treat it as prototype-only until rights are confirmed.
- **Open government data:** The Valuer-General sales and Homes Victoria rents catalogue records list CC BY 4.0 [19][20]. Some Vicmap products reportedly ban on-selling, so check each dataset's licence.
- **Advice regulation:** an AFS licence is needed to give financial product advice [25]. ASIC says advising on property through a self-managed super fund is financial product advice [26]. A search summary (unverified) says direct real property is not a financial product. Growth projections were not tested against this. Keep the report to factual information, avoid recommendations to buy, and get legal advice before adding forecasts.
- **Not found:** Victorian rules on non-agents showing "estimated value", rules for calling agents (Spam Act, Do Not Call), consumer-law guidance on forecasts.

## Cross-dimension insights

1. **The free building page changes the whole angle.** By our internal comparison, our mock-up's strongest sections duplicate Domain. The honest differentiators are the layers nobody free shows (building finances, short-stay) plus synthesis. That is a smaller, more defensible product than "comps for everything".
2. **The best-evidenced pain is also the hardest to serve.** Building financial health is the documented gap [28] but the source documents sit with owners corporation managers, the certificate price is capped at $166.48 to $299.63 [9], and Cohabit says it is already there (unverified) [11]. Short-stay data we already have; building data we would have to buy or build per building.
3. **Our data source is the weak link.** The REA wrapper misses recent sales Domain lists and has unclear rights, while Domain gives the same view free to consumers. A paid product should use licensed or government sources for anything it sells.
4. **Growth history is free at both suburb and building level.** Official suburb medians lag by about six months [19], and Domain's building page already shows per-unit growth [1]. Four recent resales in 628 Flinders St lost 1.6% to 3.0% a year over ten-year holds (one building, small sample). Honest growth content is history, not forecasts.

## Contrary evidence

The red-team pass was off at this validation level. The nearest counter-evidence is in the findings above: free building data exists [1]; brokers already have a bundled report [6]; most buyers reportedly skip even cheap reports [30] (weak evidence), which suggests low willingness to pay. A dedicated red-team pass on the "building and short-stay layer" recommendation is advisable before committing.

## Addendum: the investor lens and the "all data in one place" idea (added 2026-11-11)

**Is Domain's website data available through the Domain API?** Mostly the ingredients, not the Building Profile. Neither Domain specification (25 v2 paths, 87 v1 paths) has a building or strata endpoint [42]. But the pieces an investor wants are there: property sales history and listing history, price and rental estimates, an AVM, a sold-listings search, comparable-property search, property features (pool, gym, balcony and more), flood, bushfire and heritage flags, schools, demographics, and a **monthly suburb performance series by property type and bedroom count** (median sold price, price percentiles, number sold, days on market, discount to asking, rental listings, mean rental yield) [42]. The Building Profile's owner/renter split and owner tenure have no matching endpoint; how Domain derives them is unknown.

**Access and conditions [43]:** corporate email required; packages agreed with an account manager; pricing tiers by usage, none published. Go-live conditions: show the "powered by Domain" logo, link back to the original listing, do not put agent contact **or price estimates behind a paywall**, and avoid storing listing data. These shape a paid product: Domain's estimates would have to be shown free, with our paid value in the analysis around them. The Terms page itself did not load and was not read.

**What drives an apartment's price (our data, 599 recent inner-Melbourne sales; R² 0.74; typical unexplained spread about 19%) [44]:** a second bedroom adds about 30%, a second bathroom about 25%, a car space about 24%, and a third bedroom about double a one-bedder. **Floor level barely matters** (floors 30+ about +7%, others within noise). By suburb, Port Melbourne sits about 22% above the CBD and Docklands about 9%. These are associations from a small, recent sample with no view, age or building-quality data. The 19% spread means same-building comps still matter.

**"Selling power" in a building [44]:** in the Domain page for 628 Flinders St, 7 of the 21 for-sale entries (33%) are the same layout as unit 310, so a seller competes with many identical units. Their visible asking ranges start at $370,000 to $390,000 ("Offers Welcome") against a last sale of $445,000. This is the kind of building-level signal an investor can use to negotiate, and no free page we read states it as a metric.

| Where else to invest? | Suburb-by-suburb screen: Domain suburb series for any suburb [42]; free alternative = Valuer-General medians plus Homes Victoria rents for every Victorian suburb [19][38] | Not built; rent side loaded for all suburbs, price side needs the Valuer-General files |
| Risks | Planning overlays (Vicmap), flood/heritage/bushfire flags (Domain [42]), owners corporation and short-stay (gaps) | Overlays done; OC and short-stay are the gaps |
| Demand: people and schools | Domain demographics and schools [42] | Not loaded; not part of this run |

**Is aggregation itself the value?** Plausible but unproven. The free tools each cover a slice (Domain building page, ANZ report, Landchecker planning) and no source we read joins them with building finances, short-stay and an alternatives screen. We found no evidence of anyone paying for the joined-up view. It is a hypothesis to test with interviews.

**Growth projection stance.** A tool that lets the user enter their own growth rate and shows resulting value, equity and cash flow over time is an illustration, not a forecast. Defaults should be historical measured growth (suburb and building), clearly labelled. No forecast is published by us (see D4 and D6).

**Other users.** A **renter** version is cheap to serve from data we hold: asking rent against the building median and the official suburb median, bond, days advertised, layout and amenities. An **owner-occupier** version would emphasise owner/renter mix, schools, transport and building condition; transport and schools are not loaded. Demand for either was not researched.
